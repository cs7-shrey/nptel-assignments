QUESTION 1: [1 mark] 
State True or False. 
In the Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) model, the surrounding context words are used to predict 
the centre target word. 
a. True 
b. False 
Correct Answer: a 
Solution: CBOW aggregates the context window and predicts the centre word, while Skip-gram does 
the reverse - it uses the centre word to predict each surrounding context word. 
 
QUESTION 2: [1 mark] 
Two distinct words in a vocabulary are represented as one-hot vectors. What is the cosine similarity 
between them? 
a. 1 
b. 0 
c. -1 
d. It depends on the size of the vocabulary 
Correct Answer: b 
Solution: One-hot vectors for distinct words have their non-zero entries at different indices, so their 
dot product is 0. The cosine similarity is therefore 0 for every pair of distinct words, which is exactly 
why one-hot encoding carries no notion of semantic similarity. 
 
QUESTION 3: [1 mark] 
A toy corpus has an initial base vocabulary of 8 unique characters. If the Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) 
algorithm performs exactly 5 merge operations, what is the final size of the vocabulary? 
a. 8 
b. 5 
c. 13 
d. 40 
Correct Answer: c 
Solution: Each merge operation adds exactly one new token to the vocabulary and the base 
characters are retained, so the final size is 8 + 5 = 13. 
 
QUESTION 4: [1 mark] 
Which statement correctly describes how WordPiece chooses the next pair to merge, compared 
with BPE? 
a. BPE merges the most frequent pair, while WordPiece merges the pair maximising 
Frequency(A, B) / (Frequency(A) x Frequency(B)) 
b. BPE merges the least frequent pair, while WordPiece merges the most frequent pair 
c. Both merge the pair with the highest raw co-occurrence frequency 
d. WordPiece merges the pair maximising Frequency(A) x Frequency(B) / Frequency(A, B) 
Correct Answer: a 
Solution: BPE is purely frequency-driven. WordPiece normalises the pair frequency by the 
frequencies of the individual tokens, so a pair is merged only when the two pieces co-occur more 
than their individual frequencies would suggest. 
 
QUESTION 5: [1 mark] 
Which of the following statements about subword tokenization are true? 
a. It guarantees that every word in the test set is present in the vocabulary as a single token. 
b. Rare and morphologically complex words are split into meaningful subword units. 
c. It offers a middle ground between word-level and character-level tokenization. 
d. It keeps the vocabulary size bounded while still being able to represent unseen words. 
Correct Answer: b, c, d 
Solution: Option (a) is false - subword tokenizers explicitly allow a word to be represented by a 
sequence of several tokens; that is how they handle unseen words with a bounded vocabulary. 
 
QUESTION 6: [2 marks] 
Consider the following word-context count matrix: 
 
 c1 c2 c3 c4 c5 
w1 2 0 3 1 4 
w2 1 3 0 2 2 
w3 4 1 2 0 3 
 
Compute the cosine similarity between w1 and w2. 
a. 0.516 
b. 0.867 
c. 0.400 
d. 0.712 
Correct Answer: a 
Solution: Dot product: (2)(1) + (0)(3) + (3)(0) + (1)(2) + (4)(2) = 2 + 0 + 0 + 2 + 8 = 12 
||w1|| = sqrt(4 + 0 + 9 + 1 + 16) = sqrt(30) = 5.4772 
||w2|| = sqrt(1 + 9 + 0 + 4 + 4) = sqrt(18) = 4.2426 
cos(w1, w2) = 12 / (5.4772 x 4.2426) = 12 / 23.238 = 0.516 
 
QUESTION 7: [1 mark] 
Why is Word2Vec training described as self-supervised? 
a. The training labels are produced by human annotators before training begins. 
b. The corpus itself supplies the labels, since the context of a word acts as its supervision signal. 
c. The model supervises its own learning rate schedule during training. 
d. An external labelled dataset is required to validate every update. 
Correct Answer: b 
Solution: No manual annotation is needed. Positive training pairs are read directly off the raw text 
using a sliding context window, so the data generates its own supervision. 
 
QUESTION 8: [1 mark] 
What is the purpose of negative sampling in Word2Vec training? 
a. To reduce the dimensionality of the learned word vectors 
b. To guarantee that gradient descent converges to a global optimum 
c. To avoid computing the softmax normalisation over the entire vocabulary 
d. To remove stop words from the training corpus 
Correct Answer: c 
Solution: The full softmax requires a sum over every word in the vocabulary at each update. 
Negative sampling replaces this with a small number of binary logistic decisions against randomly 
drawn negative words, making each update cheap and independent of vocabulary size. 
 
QUESTION 9: [1 mark] 
Which of the following representations handles Out-Of-Vocabulary (OOV) words best? 
a. One-hot encoding 
b. Count-based SVD embeddings 
c. CBOW Word2Vec 
d. FastText embeddings 
Correct Answer: d 
Solution: FastText represents a word as the sum of its character n-gram vectors, so an embedding 
can be composed for a word never seen during training. The other three assign vectors only to 
words present in the training vocabulary.
